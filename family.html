For family and caregivers

You can see his glucose on your own phone.

It updates every five minutes. You can set your own alarm so your phone wakes you if he goes low overnight. It takes about ten minutes to set up and it is free.

Setting it up

  1. He turns on Share.On his phone, in the Dexcom G7 app: Connections, then Share, then switch it on. This needs his phone. The handheld receiver on its own cannot share.
  2. He invites you by email.Tap Invite Follower, enter your name and email address. He chooses whether you see just the number, or the graph as well.
  3. You install Dexcom Follow.Open his invitation email on your phone and tap the link. Follow is a separate free app. You do not need the main Dexcom app.
  4. Accept within 7 days.Invitations expire after a week. If yours lapses, he can tap Resend Invitation.
  5. Set your own alarms.Tap the gear icon next to his name. Set a Low, a High, and a No Data alert. Give each one a delay so a brief dip does not wake you for nothing.

The one rule that matters

Never treat from the Follow app. What you see is always older than what he sees. If his number looks alarming on your phone, call him and have him look at his own screen. His device makes the decisions. Yours is a smoke detector, not a thermostat.

This is Dexcom's own instruction, not caution on my part. The manual says the information followers get "isn't meant to be used for treatment decisions, analysis, or teaching."

What the alerts mean when they reach you

Urgent LowHe is at 55 mg/dL or below right now. Fixed at 55 for followers and cannot be changed. This is the one to act on.
LowHe is at or below the level you chose, usually 70. He most likely already knows: his own device alarmed first.
HighAt or above your chosen level. Rarely urgent. If he has taken insulin in the last hour or two, the correct thing is usually to wait.
No DataNothing is arriving. Usually a flat phone, no internet, or he has walked away from his phone. It does not mean something happened to him.

Both apps have to be running, both phones need internet, and both need charge. If his phone is dead you see nothing, and that is normal rather than a fault.

When to call him, and when to call for help

Call him

Urgent Low, or a low that is not coming back up after a while. Ask him to look at his own screen and do a finger stick. Ask whether he has treated it.

Call emergency services

If he is confused, cannot be roused, cannot swallow safely, or is having a seizure. Do not wait to see if the number recovers, and do not try to give food or drink to someone who is not fully alert.

Call Dexcom, not the doctor

Equipment problems: a sensor that failed early, a patch that fell off, an error code. They answer 24 hours a day and replacements are routine.

1-888-738-3646

Call his doctor

Anything clinical: changing an alarm level, changing a dose, a site that is red, swollen, or badly itchy, or a pattern you are worried about. Alarm levels are a prescription decision, not a preference.

Two things worth knowing about him

A follower alarm at three in the morning is frightening for you, and a phone call is frightening for him. Agree in advance what you will do, so neither of you is improvising at night.

And go easy on the number of alerts you set. If your phone cries wolf every evening you will start ignoring it, which is exactly when it matters. Set the Low a little lower than you think, and add a delay.
